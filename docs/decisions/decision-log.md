Architecture unchanged: the same eleven storyboard sections, in the same order, with the same copy.

## 2026-07-27 — Spacing + motion pass

| # | Decision | Rationale | Source |
|---|----------|-----------|--------|
| 22 | **Design tokens must be consumed as `var(--token)`** inside Tailwind arbitrary values, never as a bare custom-property name | In Tailwind v4 the bare bracket form is a *literal* arbitrary value and emits a declaration whose value is the property name — invalid CSS, dropped silently with no error or warning. Six tokens were dead in production: every section rendered at **0px** vertical padding, `--container-content` never capped content (1425px at a 1440 viewport), the header collapsed to 48.75px against a mobile nav panel that assumed 4.5rem, and button/link transitions were 0s. Also broke `scroll-mt` on `#join`, so that anchor landed under the sticky header. Fixed in 7 usages across 6 files and pinned by `tests/e2e/tokens.spec.ts`. | Tailwind v4 arbitrary-value syntax; WCAG 2.2 AA 2.4.11 (focus not obscured) |
| 23 | Section rhythm raised to `clamp(4.5rem, 3rem + 5vw, 9rem)`, plus a `size="lg"` variant at `clamp(6rem, 4rem + 6vw, 12rem)` | 72px mobile / 120px desktop, with the opening and closing bands at 96/150px so spacing varies for rhythm instead of being uniform. The brand character is "spacious, editorial"; the previous scale was never actually visible because of #22. | `frontend.md` design character |
| 24 | Scroll reveals are **transform-only and never animate opacity** | The first implementation faded 0 → 1 and had two measured failure modes that both shipped blank content: arming the hidden state made copy visibly fade *out* after load (caught at opacity 0.046), and a missed IntersectionObserver callback left elements at opacity 0 permanently — three of four reveals were still invisible after a full scroll-through. Transform-only makes the worst case "readable but offset by 1.5rem" instead of "invisible", and a 6s safety timer settles anything the observer misses. Applied to 4 moments, not all 11 sections. Pinned by `tests/e2e/reveal.spec.ts`. | Impeccable motion rules; `frontend.md` (no gratuitous animation) |
| 25 | **No AI-generated video** on the site (Kling or otherwise), despite it being requested | AI imagery is approved as "conceptual/editorial only — never documentary proof". A still of a woman on a cliff reads as mood; video of her reads as footage of a real client on a real trip, which is exactly the claim Charter §18 forbids. Secondary: images ship as 24–64KB AVIF today and a hero video loop is 1–5MB. Motion is CSS-only, no new dependencies. | Charter §18; project asset rules |

## 2026-07-27 — Depth and motion pass

The page was reported as reading "flat". Measuring it identified the cause, and it was not a
missing background effect: **all ten section headings rendered at the identical 42.88px**, **one of
eleven sections was full-bleed**, and **Copper appeared on exactly one element** in the page body.

| # | Decision | Rationale | Source |
|---|----------|-----------|--------|
| 26 | **No shaders / WebGL**, despite being asked to consider them | three.js + react-three-fiber is ~600KB+ against a total of 102KB across four dependencies — 6x the bundle for decoration. A procedural background also reads as "AI startup", which is the second-order reflex (`editorial brand needs depth → add a shader`), and it competes with the photography, which is the brand's strongest asset. 21st.dev was reviewed as requested; its headline components (Spline Scene, Container Scroll Animation, Spotlight Card, Radial Orbital Timeline) are shadcn/framer-motion in a SaaS idiom. Two *patterns* were borrowed and implemented natively; no code and no dependency taken. | `frontend.md` (minimal third-party JS, no gratuitous animation) |
| 27 | Type scale gains `--text-statement` and `--text-h2-sm`, exposed as a `size` prop on `SectionIntro` | Two heading sizes across eleven sections is no dynamic range, and it was the single largest cause of the flatness. Statement weight goes to the two narrative beats (the Method, the final CTA); the quiet step goes to utility sections (Travel Notes, newsletter). Visual weight is now independent of heading level — semantics stay `h2`. Result: **4 distinct heading sizes** (72/60/44/32 at 1440). | `frontend.md` design character |
| 28 | The community band becomes the page's **one full-bleed break**, built from primitives rather than `<Section>` | Ten of eleven bands repeated the same 1152px box. This one goes edge to edge with the copy in a solid panel overlapping upward into the photograph — depth from layering rather than from an effect. Legibility comes from the opaque panel, never from the image, so it holds whichever photograph is placed there. Bespoke's image also bleeds into the gutter at `lg` so one more composition escapes the box. | Brand register (image-led briefs) |
| 29 | Motion moves to **CSS scroll-driven animation**; `Reveal.tsx` and its `"use client"` boundary are deleted | `animation-timeline: view()` is at ~84% support and degrades to the settled state. It is a function of scroll position, so there is no event to miss — which removes the failure mode measured in the observer version, where a fast scroll left three of four reveals stranded. Still transform-only, never opacity. **Trap worth recording:** `.editorial-image` and the hero `<section>` both set `overflow: hidden`, which makes them scroll containers, so an anonymous `view()`/`scroll()` on a child resolved against those non-scrolling boxes and pinned progress at a constant 50%. Fixed with *named* timelines (`view-timeline-name`) declared on the frame. | Impeccable motion rules |
| 30 | Copper reintroduced in three deliberate places | It was on one element. Now: the Method eyebrow rule, a hairline marking the full-bleed break, and the signature rule closing the final CTA — plus the secondary-button border on hover. Still decorative only: it never carries body text or status meaning, and the Copper-on-Olive rule in the CTA is not required to clear AA. | `frontend.md` palette roles; WCAG 1.4.1 |

| 39 | Track trimmed 260vh → 220vh | At 260vh the page measured 13,106px, over the ~13,000px scroll-length ceiling set in the plan. | 40 | Band spacing gains a third step (`sm`), hero to `100svh`, display type to the 6rem ceiling | Uniform band height was the core problem. | 41 | **The hero video is removed** — every piece of motion now answers to scroll | The client asked whether the site could work like a scroll-scrubbed animation rather than a running movie. The video was the sole exception — 6.3MB on its own clock, and precisely what made the hero feel like footage playing rather than a composition responding to the visitor. Removing it also drops the last unnecessary client boundary (back to two) and **improved** hero contrast from 5.34:1 to **6.25:1**, because a still under a fixed scrim is more predictable than video under one. | Client direction |
| 42 | Video scrubbing and object-disassembly both **rejected** | MP4 seeking is not frame-accurate — it jumps between keyframes and stutters; smooth scrubbing needs an all-intra re-encode at ~20–30MB. Separately, the watch-teardown reference does not transfer: a watch works because it is a manufactured object whose parts are the story. This brand sells travel planning — there is no object, and content-safety forbids animating a fabricated itinerary assembling itself. A teardown would be the "gratuitous animation" the frontend rules ban. | `frontend.md`; Charter §18 |
| 43 | The hero's own elements separate on exit instead | The "parts moving" gesture applied to composition rather than to a product. Four depths on the existing `--hero-frame` timeline: eyebrow travels −6rem, CTAs −1.25rem. Verified bidirectional — identical offsets at identical scroll positions in both directions. **Two animations require two elements:** `animation` is a single property, and the first attempt put the scroll lift on the same elements as the load entrance, where it silently lost a specificity fight with `.hero-enter > div > *` (0,1,1 beats 0,1,0) and never ran while looking correct in the stylesheet. The lift now sits on wrapper divs and the entrance one level inside. | — |
| 44 | The journey thread is **per-band, not page-length** | A single line drawn 0→1 on `scroll()` over the full page is a progress bar rotated ninety degrees — it reports "you are 47% done" rather than saying anything about a journey. Each band draws its own segment on its own view timeline, chaining visually without carrying global completion state. Quiet by design: 1px Copper at 45%, desktop only, in the gutter outside the content measure. Under reduced motion it lands fully drawn, never half — a partial line implies broken progress. | `frontend.md` (editorial rule/margin system) |
| 45 | Two measurement bugs corrected, both mine | `timeline-scope` was declared alongside `view-timeline-name` on every band, which made the name ambiguous; descendants already see an ancestor's timeline, so it was removed. The same class of error as reading `"0px 40px"` with `/^0px/`. Both now read the property that is actually animated. | — |
| 46 | **Scroll-scrubbed image sequence** in the Reigate story section — she walks down the steps as the page descends | The client asked for the woman in the arched-doorway still to descend the stairs on scroll. The still is ideal for it: she is already mid-stride, back to camera, heading down toward the water, so descending the page descends the steps. Generated with Kling from that exact still, prompted for a locked-off camera because any camera drift makes extracted frames inconsistent and the scrub reads as a wobble. Verified frames 0→23 advance her down the steps with the archway perfectly still. | Client direction |
| 47 | Frames extracted **without ffmpeg**, via headless Chromium | ffmpeg is not installed and I would not install a system package unasked. | 48 | Sprite strip + `steps()` on a scroll timeline, **not** video scrubbing and **no JavaScript** | MP4 seeking is not frame-accurate, so `currentTime` scrubbing jumps between keyframes and stutters. Bidirectional for free: verified frames 0→4→10→17→23 descending and symmetrically back. Weight: source clip 14MB → strip **538KB** at quality 58 (24 frames only ever seen one at a time and in motion, so artefacts that would show in a static hero are invisible).
